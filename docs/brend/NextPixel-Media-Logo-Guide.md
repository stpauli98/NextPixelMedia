# NextPixel Media — Logo & Visual Identity Development Guide

> **Status:** Working brand specification / source of truth for further logo development  
> **Brand:** NextPixel Media  
> **Relationship:** Sister brand of NextPixel.dev  
> **Selected logo direction:** **NP Monogram — Variant 3**  
> **Purpose:** Give future designers, developers, AI agents, and branding tools enough context to continue the identity consistently without re-inventing the direction.

---

## 1. Brand architecture

NextPixel Media is part of the broader NextPixel brand family.

### Parent brand logic

- **NextPixel.dev** = digital products, software, websites, automation, SaaS, technical systems
- **NextPixel Media** = photography, video production, events, commercial content, aerial/drone, visual storytelling

The two brands should clearly look related, but they should not look identical.

### Core visual distinction

| Brand | Main character | Main accent |
|---|---|---|
| NextPixel.dev | Technical, precise, digital, product-oriented | Teal |
| NextPixel Media | Cinematic, premium, visual, emotional | Champagne gold |

The visual identity must retain a recognizable **NextPixel DNA** while giving Media a more cinematic and premium character.

---

## 2. Selected logo direction

The selected concept is **Variant 3 — NP Monogram**.

### Core idea

The logo consists of:

- a geometric **N** in white / light cream
- a stylized **P** in champagne gold
- both letters visually integrated into a compact **NP monogram**
- four corner marks around the monogram, inspired by:
  - camera framing
  - focus guides
  - crop marks
  - viewfinder UI
- optional small circular focus markers / dots near the corner marks

### Desired visual character

The selected monogram should feel:

- modern
- cinematic
- premium
- clean
- memorable
- slightly dynamic
- photography/video-adjacent without using a literal camera icon
- suitable for professional event, commercial, wedding, sport, real-estate, and corporate work

### Important constraint

Do **not** turn the mark into a generic photography logo.

Avoid obvious visual clichés such as:

- camera body icons
- aperture / shutter symbols as the primary mark
- drone silhouettes
- film reels
- lens illustrations
- clapperboards
- wedding rings or heart symbols

The identity should communicate **media production**, not merely "photographer".

---

## 3. Monogram construction intent

Future designers/agents should preserve the visual logic of Variant 3 rather than copying AI-generation imperfections literally.

### N

- clean geometric sans-serif construction
- white or Soft White on dark backgrounds
- dark navy/black on light backgrounds
- should feel stable and structural
- avoid overly thin strokes

### P

- champagne gold
- can share/interlock visually with the right side of the N
- should remain readable as a `P`
- may use a subtle custom cut or diagonal interaction with the N
- must not become overly decorative

### Interaction between N and P

The letters should feel like one intentional symbol rather than two letters simply placed next to each other.

Preferred characteristics:

- slight overlap or shared geometry
- optical balance around the vertical center
- compact enough for avatar/favicons
- recognizable at small sizes
- no unnecessary micro-details inside the letters

---

## 4. Frame / focus marks

The four outer corners are an essential part of the selected concept.

### Function

They visually connect the logo to:

- photography
- cinematography
- framing
- focus
- composition

without becoming a literal camera symbol.

### Style

- champagne gold
- thin to medium stroke
- right-angle corner geometry
- positioned symmetrically around the NP monogram
- enough negative space between monogram and frame

### Focus dots

Variant 3 includes small circular markers near the framing corners.

These are optional secondary details.

Use them when:

- mark is displayed at medium/large size
- video intro/outro
- print/signage
- social avatar if still readable

Remove them when:

- favicon is very small
- watermark is tiny
- legibility becomes worse

The corner frame is more important than the dots.

## 5. Primary color palette

### Core colors

| Name | HEX | Main use |
|---|---:|---|
| **Cinematic Black** | `#0B1018` | Main dark background, cinematic surfaces |
| **NextPixel Navy** | `#1E3A5F` | Brand-family connection, secondary backgrounds/details |
| **Champagne Gold** | `#C6A96B` | Media accent, P in monogram, frame marks, premium detail |
| **Warm Cream** | `#F8F6F0` | Light background, editorial layouts |
| **Soft White** | `#F5F5F3` | Main text and N on dark surfaces |
| **Muted Gray** | `#8A919B` | Secondary text, captions, metadata |
| **Pure White** | `#FFFFFF` | Utility white where maximum contrast is needed |

### Primary combination

The default NextPixel Media combination should be:

- `#0B1018` — background
- `#F5F5F3` — N / main typography
- `#C6A96B` — P / frame / accent

### Color hierarchy

Approximate usage ratio:

- **70%** dark backgrounds / photography
- **20%** cream / white / neutral surfaces
- **10%** champagne accent

Champagne should remain an **accent**, not become a full gold-luxury aesthetic.

Avoid large gold gradients, excessive metallic effects, and ornamental luxury styling.

## 9. Typography

### Existing NextPixel family typography

NextPixel.dev uses:

- **Montserrat** for headings
- **Poppins** for body text

NextPixel Media should retain this connection by default.

### Recommended use

#### Montserrat

Use for:

- logo exploration
- navigation
- labels
- service names
- social overlays
- metadata
- uppercase `MEDIA`
- modern/corporate layouts

Recommended weights:

- 500
- 600
- 700

#### Poppins

Use for:

- body copy
- descriptions
- website text
- captions
- service explanations

Recommended weights:

- 300
- 400
- 500

### Optional editorial serif

A secondary serif may be introduced for selected large editorial headings and cinematic campaigns, but **not as a replacement for the main NextPixel typography system**.

Possible candidates:

- Cormorant Garamond
- DM Serif Display

Use only for:

- large campaign headlines
- wedding/editorial creative
- premium story-driven content

Do not use the serif in UI, body copy, or technical/corporate layouts.

## 10. Visual style principles

NextPixel Media should feel:

- dark-mode dominant
- cinematic
- minimal
- editorial
- premium without looking luxurious-for-the-sake-of-luxury
- modern rather than classic
- confident without being loud

### Photography-first rule

**Real photography and video are the primary visual content.**

Branding should support the media, not compete with it.

Preferred ratio for portfolio content:

- 85–95% image/video
- 5–15% typography/logo/brand elements

Avoid oversized frames and heavy graphic templates over strong photography.

### Portfolio posts

- full-bleed photo/video
- minimal branding
- small watermark when needed
- no unnecessary cards or graphic boxes

### Promotional posts

- Cinematic Black background

Can borrow more structure from NextPixel.dev:

## 12. Watermark rules

### Preferred watermark

Use the **NP monogram** or a simplified single-color version.

### Placement

Default:

- bottom-right
- generous inset from image edge
- low opacity

Recommended opacity:

- 25–50%, depending on image

### Rule

Watermark must never distract from the photograph.

For premium portfolio/editorial images, no watermark may be preferable.

## 14. Logo clear space

Until exact vector geometry is finalized, use this provisional rule:

- clear space around the NP monogram = at least **25% of the monogram width**
- no text or other elements should enter this area

For the framed monogram, clear space is measured from the outer focus/crop marks, not from the letters.

## 18. What NOT to do

Do not:

- distort the logo
- use random gold shades
- replace champagne with bright yellow
- use gradients inside the final core logo unless explicitly approved
- add shadows/glows to the master logo asset
- place the logo on visually noisy backgrounds without contrast control
- redesign the P into an aperture symbol
- make the frame marks overly thick
- overuse the focus dots
- use ornate luxury-serif typography everywhere
- add generic camera/drone icons next to the logo
- use NextPixel.dev teal as the dominant Media accent

Mockups may use lighting/metallic effects, but **master logo files must stay flat and clean**.

---

## 19. AI-generated concept disclaimer

The current chosen Variant 3 originated from AI-assisted concept exploration.

Important:

- treat it as a **design direction**, not production-ready vector artwork
- do not trace AI artifacts blindly
- reconstruct the monogram precisely in Figma / Illustrator / Affinity Designer
- correct optical alignment, stroke widths, spacing, curves, and symmetry manually

The final production logo should be deterministic and reproducible.

---

## 20. Recommended vectorization workflow

### Step 1 — Rebuild the N

- use a clean geometric sans reference
- convert to vector outlines if necessary
- adjust stroke geometry manually

### Step 2 — Build/customize the P

- ensure the P reads clearly
- match optical weight with N
- create the intentional overlap/interlock

### Step 3 — Resolve monogram balance

Test:

- centered alignment
- optical center, not only mathematical center
- whitespace between strokes
- N/P visual hierarchy

### Step 4 — Build frame corners

- use consistent stroke width
- symmetric corner lengths
- consistent distance from monogram

### Step 5 — Add focus dots

- test both with and without dots
- create full and simplified versions

### Step 6 — Test in real contexts

Test the mark on:

- Instagram avatar
- website navbar
- photo watermark
- dark video intro
- cream business card
- equipment sticker
- 32×32 favicon

### Step 7 — Export SVG master files

All SVGs should:

- use clean paths
- avoid embedded raster content
- avoid unnecessary masks
- avoid external font dependencies for final logo
- preserve exact brand HEX values

## 24. Evaluation criteria for future logo iterations

Every new variation should be judged against these questions:

1. Is `NP` recognizable immediately?
2. Does it still look good at 32–64 px?
3. Does it feel related to NextPixel.dev?
4. Does it feel more cinematic than technical?
5. Is it premium without becoming a wedding-luxury cliché?
6. Does the frame communicate media/focus without looking like a generic camera icon?
7. Does the champagne accent remain restrained?
8. Does it work on both dark and cream backgrounds?
9. Can it be reproduced cleanly as SVG?
10. Would it still make sense for weddings, sport, events, commercial shoots, real estate, and drone work?

Reject concepts that score poorly on versatility or small-size legibility even if they look impressive in large mockups.

## 25. Short brief for future AI/design agents

> **Design/refine the NextPixel Media identity using the selected Variant 3 NP monogram direction. Preserve the wider NextPixel brand family through Montserrat/Poppins, navy heritage, clean geometry, and minimal composition. Make Media distinct through Cinematic Black (`#0B1018`) and Champagne Gold (`#C6A96B`). The NP monogram should use a white/light N, champagne P, and four camera-viewfinder-inspired corner marks with optional focus dots. Avoid literal camera, lens, aperture, drone, or wedding clichés. The final logo must be modern, cinematic, premium, versatile, highly legible at small sizes, and production-ready as clean SVG vectors. Treat existing AI-generated imagery as concept reference only; rebuild geometry manually and consistently.**

## 27. Final design principle

> **NextPixel.dev builds the digital experience. NextPixel Media captures and presents the visual experience.**

The brands should feel like two specialized branches of the same NextPixel system.

NextPixel Media should be recognizable as NextPixel — but it should feel like it belongs behind a camera, on a film set, at an event, or inside a premium visual campaign rather than inside a software dashboard.
